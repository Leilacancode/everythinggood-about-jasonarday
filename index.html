Everything good about Jason Arday

We are tired of the single reductionist narrative about the kindest person we knew. So we're gathering everything good about Jason Arday, in the words of the people who were there.

In memory of Jason Arday, 1985 - 2026

This site is built by Professor Jason Arday's former students.

We're collecting first-hand testimonies from people who knew him, learned from him, or crossed paths with him. Supervisions and seminars, emails, a reading list, a word of advice that stuck. Small things count. Small things are usually the whole point.

This isn't a place to debate the events of recent months, and we won't publish anything that is disrespectful to him and his family. Every submission here is read by one of us before it appears.

Testimonies

Jason stopped me after class to tell me I should become an academic. At that time, I was rejected from a PhD application, I wasn't sure if I should keep trying

He told me "it's their loss" and was willing to supervise me himself just to ensure I don't give up.

I'm doing my PhD elsewhere now. I always assumed I'd run into him at a conference one day and get to tell him. I'm so sad he isn't here to witness my growth, my journey is a trajectory he set off, I hope I'd have made him proud.

Leila Lai
Jason's MPhil student, 2025

I met Jason in my first year as a grad student at Cambridge, when he visited the Faculty before becoming Professor. At the time I felt exhausted as one of the few black students at the Faculty and felt pretty isolated. When I saw him in the Faculty his smile was like a warm light in a cold winter. We had a chat about Blackness and Cambridge and I remember him telling me how important it is for us as minority academics to stick together. It was a short interaction but one that was significant - so reassuring to see a face like mine, especially one that was so kind and confident in this space. He really gave me hope that I could make it here too.

Thank you Jason.

Anon
PhD Student

I first met Jason when he joined the faculty. As a Professor of Sociology, his work directly intersected with my own research in sociology and higher education. Over several conversations, we spoke about the complexities of institutional change, what makes it difficult, and what truly makes it work. He always showed genuine appreciation for my involvement with the Faculty of Education Research Student Association (FERSA), believing deeply in the role student leadership plays in creating sustainable change. In a large academic institution, he made me feel seen.

During my term as FERSA President from 2023 to 2024, Jason was selected as the keynote speaker for our annual conference. Watching him speak to our community was inspiring, but what stayed with me most was what he shared afterward. He told me that he saw my potential in the field because I truly cared. As a person of color navigating the field of sociology, seeing his leadership and hearing his belief in me gave me the confidence to believe that I have a chance in academia, regardless of the social constraints.

Jason once shared two insights that I will carry with me forever. He said that sociology is a discipline enabled by love and community. He also spoke about his commitment to teaching, telling me that he strived to give his absolute best to other people’s children in the classroom, hoping that others would do the same for his own children one day.

I am heartbroken to learn of his passing so soon after my graduation. I never had the opportunity to reach out as an alumna to share where my journey has led, but I will forever remember Jason as a remarkable scholar, a devoted teacher, and a compassionate mentor.

Thank you, Jason, for your unwavering kindness, your belief in us, and your tireless efforts to create real change. You will be deeply missed.

Rossa Darni
PhD Candidate

I was unsure about how to approach my dissertation which was on a challenging topic (grief education). Specifically, I couldn’t decide whether it was professional to include a personal narrative to preface the introduction. I stayed behind after class and asked Jason his thoughts. He reassured me that all research comes from a perspective and we write with it whether we address it or not. I’m fairly certain his advice to include it was what ultimately secured me a distinction for my dissertation (graded by two other lecturers), because this element was explicitly commented on positively. I’m grateful to Jason for being such an attentive and passionate lecturer. Students don’t always feel seen at Cambridge and many other lecturers had a distaste for teaching us, but he was talented teacher and I felt uniquely seen and understood by him.

Laura Johnson
MPhil student,2025
